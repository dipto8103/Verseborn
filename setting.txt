**I. Overall Scene Composition & Lighting:**

*   **Environment Lighting:**
    *   **Type:** Subsurface Scattering with volumetric effects. Simulate filtered sunlight penetrating the water.
    *   **Color:** A gradient ranging from a bright, warm yellow-orange near the top (simulating sunlight) to a cooler, deeper blue-green at the bottom. Use a "Light Path" node to control color based on ray depth.
    *   **Intensity:** Moderate to allow good visibility while still suggesting depth. Adjust scattering and absorption coefficients within the volume material for desired light diffusion.
    *   **Shadows:** Soft, diffused shadows are critical. Consider using a large area light source ("Area" light type) positioned above the scene to mimic the sun. Enable "Contact Shadows" for subtle grounding.
*   **Water Simulation:**
    *   **Depth:** Assume a scene depth of at least 20-30 Blender units (meters).
    *   **Turbidity:** Moderate turbidity. Fine particulate matter suspended in the water. This can be achieved through a volume shader affecting light scattering and absorption.
    *   **Caustics:** Simulate light caustics on the coral and seabed. This can be done with procedural textures (Voronoi noise) influencing the normals of surfaces, or through a dedicated caustic lighting setup (more complex).
    *   **Water surface:** the water surface should be slightly distorted using noise texture, with animated parameters to create the illusion of moving water.

**II. Coral Formations:**

*   **Types:** Diverse mix of hard and soft corals. Include branching corals (Acropora), plate corals (Montipora), brain corals (Diploria), mushroom corals (Fungia), sea fans (Gorgonians), and soft corals (e.g., leather corals, finger corals).
*   **Geometry:**
    *   **Branching Corals:** Use procedural generation techniques (e.g., L-systems, branching algorithms) to create complex, organic branching structures. Start with a base cylinder or cone and iteratively add branches with varying angles and lengths. Polycount can range from 5,000 to 50,000 faces per coral, depending on complexity.
        *   **Vertices Distribution:** Denser vertex concentration at the branching points for smoother curvature.
    *   **Plate Corals:** Start with a flat, slightly curved plane. Use noise textures (e.g., Perlin noise) to add surface detail and undulations. Polycount: 2,000 - 10,000 faces.
    *   **Brain Corals:** Model as irregular, rounded shapes with deep grooves and ridges. Use sculpting tools or displacement maps to create the characteristic brain-like texture. Polycount: 10,000 - 30,000 faces.
        *   **Vertices Distribution:** High density on the upper portion, with lower density toward the ground.
    *   **Mushroom Corals:** Start with a disc shape. Use proportional editing and sculpting to create the mushroom-like form with a central stalk. Polycount: 3,000 - 15,000 faces.
    *   **Sea Fans:** Generate a lattice-like structure using curves or a skeletal mesh. Add subtle waving animations. Polycount: 5,000 to 20,000 faces.
    *   **Soft Corals:** Model as elongated, flowing shapes. Use cloth simulation or dynamic paint to simulate movement in the water. Polycount: 8,000 to 40,000 faces.
*   **Materials:**
    *   **Subsurface Scattering (SSS):** Essential for realistic coral appearance. Experiment with different SSS radii values (e.g., Red: 0.2, Green: 0.1, Blue: 0.05) to achieve a glowing, translucent effect.
    *   **Colors:** Highly saturated and diverse. Reds, oranges, yellows, greens, blues, purples. Use color ramps and procedural textures to create variations within each coral.
        *   **Color Variation:** Introduce subtle color variations based on depth and position. Deeper corals tend to be less vibrant due to light absorption.
    *   **Texture:** Use a combination of procedural textures (e.g., Voronoi, Musgrave) and image textures to add surface detail, bumps, and imperfections.
    *   **UV Unwrapping:** Crucial for applying textures effectively. Use a combination of automatic unwrapping ("Smart UV Project") and manual unwrapping where necessary.

**III. Marine Creatures:**

*   **Types:** Variety of fish (e.g., clownfish, angelfish, butterflyfish), invertebrates (e.g., starfish, sea urchins, crabs, sea snails), and possibly larger animals (e.g., sea turtles, rays).
*   **Geometry:**
    *   **Fish:** Model with smooth, streamlined bodies. Use subdivision surface modifiers to achieve a smooth, organic look. Polycount: 2,000 - 10,000 faces per fish, depending on size and detail.
        *   **Vertices Distribution:** higher density on the fins, lower on the body.
    *   **Invertebrates:** Model with appropriate anatomical detail. Use reference images for accuracy. Polycount: varies greatly depending on the creature. Starfish: 1,000 - 5,000 faces. Sea Urchins: 5,000 - 20,000 faces. Crabs: 3,000 - 15,000 faces.
    *   **Sea Turtle:** Model the shell with high details, and use a lower density on the limbs. Overall polycount: 20,000 - 50,000 faces.
*   **Materials:**
    *   **Colors:** Similar to corals, use highly saturated colors and patterns. Clownfish: orange, white, black. Angelfish: bright yellows, blues, blacks. Butterflyfish: complex patterns of stripes and spots.
    *   **Specular Highlights:** Add specular highlights to simulate wet surfaces. Use a low roughness value (e.g., 0.1 - 0.3).
    *   **Iridescence:** Consider adding subtle iridescence to certain fish scales using a "Principled BSDF" shader with a thin film layer.
*   **Rigging and Animation:**
    *   **Fish:** Rig with bones for swimming animation (body undulation, fin movements). Use shape keys for subtle facial expressions.
    *   **Sea Turtles:** Rig the flippers for swimming.

**IV. Plant Life:**

*   **Types:** Seaweed, seagrass, anemones.
*   **Geometry:**
    *   **Seaweed/Seagrass:** Model as elongated, flowing shapes. Use curve modifiers or cloth simulation to create realistic movement. Polycount: 1,000 - 5,000 faces per plant.
    *   **Anemones:** Model with tentacles. Use array modifiers to create multiple tentacles around a central body. Polycount: 5,000 - 20,000 faces.
*   **Materials:**
    *   **Colors:** Greens, browns, reds.
    *   **Translucency:** Seaweed and seagrass should have some degree of translucency to allow light to pass through.

**V. Seabed:**

*   **Geometry:** Uneven, rocky surface. Use displacement maps or sculpting to create realistic terrain. Polycount: high, depending on the area covered.
    *   **Vertices Distribution:** The distribution should be denser in areas near the coral, and can be sparser far away.
*   **Materials:** Sand, rock, silt. Use a mix of textures to create variations in color and texture.

*   **Scale:** Maintain a consistent scale throughout the scene. One Blender unit can be considered one meter.
*   **Performance:** Optimize geometry and textures to maintain good performance, especially in complex scenes with many objects. Use LOD (Level of Detail) techniques to reduce polycount for distant objects. Consider using instances for repeated objects (e.g., multiple small fish).
